--- PAGE 12 ---
Random Negative Sampling
- Yes, assuming random comparisons and saying this one is better 
than the other so we can train because jeje. 
- PROS:
- Easy; Fast;
- CONS:
- Way to easy and weird for the model. You might not 
generalise anything.



--- PAGE 13 ---
Hard Negative Sampling
- You first check your data.
- Then you must select the examples that are HARDER to choose 
between and you directly put it → Mistral vs. Baluard
- Since you are forcing the hardest options, you are basically 
ensuring it must choose in the ones where deciding is more “meh 
everything’s fine” so you can get better approaches.
- PROS:
- It makes the model better automatically. Also useful for DL.
- Realistic.
- CONS:
- Complex to do.

--- PAGE 16 ---
In-Batch Negatives (more for actual DL)
- Using other examples from the test as free negatives. So you 
never test with a single label, you test with a set of negatives. 
- Basically, if a thing happens it implies the other one is gonna 
happen!!
- If a person buys a pair of vans, it can be implied that he will buy a 
guitar and a skateboard. (JK)
- So a batch is: 
- batch = [item1, item2, item3] → Being the positives and 
negatives the other batch.



--- PAGE 17 ---
In-Batch Negatives (more for actual DL)
- It is mostly used to train “Word2Vec” and “Node2Vec” and 
“Wav2Vec”. 
- So generally good to train Transformers :) 
- IS A TRANSFORMER A RECOMMENDAITON SYSTEM=
- PROS:
- Free (not need to generate anything)
- Efficient and scalable.
- CONS:
- Negatives can be reeeeally bad.
- Not always hard.
- Depends.



--- PAGE 18 ---
Dynamic / Adversarial Negatives (Pro-level jeje)
- Super powerful. The model generates its own mistakes to learn 
from.
- We predict a thing, and from this one we are like oh wait, we 
have the opposite. We use it to train. 
- So we are basically telling the system “You are not doing it right”.
- THE FLOW:
- Model predicts the rank.
- Get the biggest mistakes.
- Convert into negatives.
- Re-train.



--- PAGE 19 ---
Dynamic / Adversarial Negatives (Pro-level jeje)
```python
candidates = model.predict(rival)
hard_negatives = top_wrong_predictions(candidates)
train(model, hard_negatives)
```
